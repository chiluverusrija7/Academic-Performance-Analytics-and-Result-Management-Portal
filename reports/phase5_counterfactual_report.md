## 1. Objective

Phase 5 builds the **Counterfactual Academic Simulator** for EduInsight AI.

While Phase 2 predicts risk probabilities, Phase 3 explains why the model made a prediction (SHAP), and Phase 4 quantifies prediction confidence (Conformal Prediction), Phase 5 provides **actionability**:
> *"What realistic, minimal change in a student's modifiable academic behavior could reduce predicted risk and transition their trajectory toward academic safety?"*

Crucially, counterfactuals are not generated by arbitrary formulaic subtraction. **Every simulated scenario modifies the student's feature vector within realistic bounds and re-evaluates it through the actual trained XGBoost pipeline, Conformal uncertainty layer, and SHAP explainer.**

```
Current Student Record (x)
  ├─ Phase 2: Risk Prob = 0.9991 (RISK)
  ├─ Phase 4: Uncertainty = CONFIDENT_RISK [{RISK}]
  └─ Phase 3: Top Risk Driver = internal_marks_avg_w12 (SHAP = +4.30)
                 │
                 ▼
       Counterfactual Engine
  (Searches Feasible Perturbations δ ∈ Modifiable Space)
                 │
                 ▼
Candidate Evaluated through Trained Pipeline: (x + δ)
  ├─ Re-evaluated Risk Prob = 0.4628 (SAFE, Δ = -0.5363)
  ├─ Re-evaluated Uncertainty = AMBIGUOUS [{SAFE}]
  └─ Re-evaluated SHAP: internal_marks_avg_w12 SHAP drops from +4.30 -> +0.00
```

## 2. Why Counterfactual Analysis is Required in Academic Decision Support

1. **Transforming Predictive Alerts into Prescriptive Guidance**: Merely notifying faculty or students that a risk score is high (e.g., $P(\text{Risk}) = 0.85$) provides diagnosis without a treatment path. Counterfactuals provide concrete behavioral benchmarks (e.g., *"Raising internal marks by +10 pts and clearing 1 failing subject is associated with dropping risk to 0.46"*).
2. **Empowering Student Agency**: Students can interact with what-if scenarios to understand the academic threshold required to alter their institutional standing.
3. **Optimizing Remedial Resource Allocation**: Academic advisors can prioritize interventions with the lowest student effort cost that reliably cross operational risk thresholds.

## 3. Modifiable vs. Non-Modifiable Feature Policy

To prevent the simulator from proposing impossible or unethical interventions, features are strictly partitioned into **Modifiable** and **Non-Modifiable (Immutable)** categories:

### A. Modifiable Academic Features (Checkpoint-Specific)
Only in-semester, actionable behavioral variables may be perturbed:
- **Week 4**: `attendance_pct_w4`, `internal_marks_avg_w4`, `low_attendance_subjects_w4`, `low_scoring_subjects_w4`.
- **Week 8**: `attendance_pct_w8`, `internal_marks_avg_w8`, `low_attendance_subjects_w8`, `low_scoring_subjects_w8`. (Dynamically updates $\Delta W4 \to W8$ deltas).
- **Week 12**: `attendance_pct_w12`, `internal_marks_avg_w12`, `low_attendance_subjects_w12`, `low_scoring_subjects_w12`. (Dynamically updates $\Delta W8 \to W12$ deltas and overall performance trends).

### B. Non-Modifiable / Immutable Features (Strictly Protected)
The simulator **strictly prohibits** manipulating:
- **Demographic & Identity Metadata**: `student_id`, `cohort_entry_year`, `dept_code`, `course_code`, `admission_category`, `fee_payment_status`.
- **Admissions & Stage Context**: `entrance_rank_percentile`, `total_registered_credits`, `semester_no`, `semester_id`, `academic_year`.
- **Historical Track Record**: `previous_sgpa`, `cumulative_cgpa_prior`, `previous_attendance_pct`, `previous_backlogs_count`, `historical_backlogs_cumulative` (cannot be retroactively altered during an active semester).
- **Downstream Outcomes / Target Leakage**: `final_sgpa`, `final_cgpa`, `final_marks_average`, `final_backlogs_count`, `final_backlog_bucket`, `final_result_classification`, `target_risk`.

## 4. Realistic Feature Bounds & Monotonicity Constraints

All perturbations $\delta$ must satisfy physical and pedagogical constraints:
1. **Physical Percentage Limits**: Attendance and internal mark averages must remain strictly within $[0.0, 100.0]\%$. A candidate requiring $105\%$ attendance is flagged as `INFEASIBLE` and rejected.
2. **Discrete Subject Bounds**: Failing subject counts (`low_scoring_subjects_*`, `low_attendance_subjects_*`) are integers bounded within $[0, 6]$.
3. **Monotonicity**: Interventions are constrained to non-negative academic improvements ($\Delta \text{Attendance} \ge 0$, $\Delta \text{Marks} \ge 0$, $\Delta \text{Failing Subjects} \le 0$).

## 5. Counterfactual Search Optimization Methodology

For an at-risk observation $x$, the simulator searches a discrete grid of structured academic scenarios:
1. **Attendance Improvement**: $\Delta \text{Att} \in \{+5\%, +10\%, +15\%, +20\%\}$
2. **Assessment Improvement**: $\Delta \text{Marks} \in \{+5, +10, +15, +20 \text{ pts}\}$
3. **Remedial Subject Clearance**: Decrementing low-scoring subject count by $1, 2, 3$ subjects with corresponding mark calibrations.
4. **Combined Multi-Faceted Scenarios**: Moderate marks ($+5$ to $+10$) + moderate attendance ($+5\%$ to $+10\%$) + clearing 1 failing subject.

### Optimization Objective
The simulator solves:
$$\min_{\delta} \text{Cost}(\delta) \quad \text{subject to} \quad \hat{P}_{\text{risk}}(x + \delta) < 0.50 \quad \text{and} \quad \text{Feasible}(x + \delta)$$
where:
$$\text{Cost}(\delta) = \frac{|\Delta \text{Att}|}{20} + \frac{|\Delta \text{Marks}|}{20} + 0.8 \cdot |\Delta \text{low\_scoring}| + 0.8 \cdot |\Delta \text{low\_attendance}|$$

- **Priority 1 (`FEASIBLE_THRESHOLD_CROSSED`)**: Feasible perturbation with the smallest intervention cost that successfully brings predicted risk below the $0.50$ decision threshold.
- **Priority 2 (`FEASIBLE_PARTIAL_REDUCTION`)**: If severe background deficits prevent crossing the $0.50$ threshold, select the feasible perturbation yielding the maximum probability reduction.
- **Priority 3 (`NO_INTERVENTION_NEEDED`)**: Filter out safe students ($P(\text{Risk}) < 0.35$ and point prediction `SAFE`) from unnecessary intervention recommendations.

## 7. In-Depth Case Analysis

### Case 1: W12 Successful Threshold Crossing via Combined Action (`STU0016`)
- **Initial State**: $P(\text{Risk}) = 99.91\%$, Attendance $= 76.37\%$, Internal Marks $= 46.09\%$, Failing Subjects $= 3$.
- **SHAP Drivers**: `internal_marks_avg_w12` ($+4.30$), `low_scoring_subjects_w12` ($+2.82$).
- **Counterfactual**: Raise attendance to $86.37\%$, marks to $56.09\%$, and remediate 1 failing subject.
- **Re-Evaluated Output**: Risk drops from **$99.91\% \to 46.28\%$** ($\Delta = -53.63\%$), shifting point prediction to `SAFE`.
- **SHAP Shift**: `internal_marks_avg_w12` attribution collapses from $+4.30 \to +0.00$.

### Case 4: W12 Chronic Multi-Subject Failure — Partial Reduction (`STU0036`)
- **Initial State**: $P(\text{Risk}) = 99.92\%$, Internal Marks $= 41.25\%$, Failing Subjects $= 4$.
- **Counterfactual**: Even with a large $+20$ points marks improvement, the model's re-evaluated risk drops to **$71.86\%$** ($\Delta = -28.06\%$), remaining above the $0.50$ threshold.
- **Pedagogical Finding**: For students with severe compound deficits across 4+ subjects, late-term single-dimensional interventions are insufficient; intensive multi-subject emergency tutoring is required.

## 8. Non-Causal Semantics and Safety Guardrails

To maintain scientific integrity, all counterfactual outputs adhere strictly to **non-causal observational phrasing**:
- **Prohibited Phrasing**: *"Improving attendance to 85% will cause the student to pass."*
- **Approved Phrasing**: *"Under the trained model, modifying attendance from 75% to 85% is associated with reducing predicted academic risk from 0.78 to 0.46, shifting the model's classification from RISK to SAFE."*

## 9. Limitations & Scientific Assumptions

1. **Model-Based Simulation vs. Real Causal DAGs**: Counterfactual predictions reflect the associative patterns learned by the XGBoost pipeline. Unobserved confounders (e.g., student illness, personal emergencies) may influence whether an attendance boost translates to exam success.
2. **Linear Grid Granularity**: The search space utilizes discrete increments (+5%, +10%, etc.). While efficient and pedagogically intuitive, continuous gradient-based counterfactual methods could discover infinitesimally smaller mathematical perturbations.
3. **Synthetic Data Context**: Simulated improvements operate on the validated Phase 1 synthetic distribution.
